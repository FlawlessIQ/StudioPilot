### F2 — "Need to add videographers" — SHIPPED (2026-09-19)
Coverage was counted in **photographers only**. `includedPhotographers` on a
package, `photographers` on the booking import, and that one number drove the
second-shooter readiness check, how many crew got offered the job, the retainer
on a per-crew package, and what the couple's portal showed. Gabe's business is
video-led — his own packages read "One Videographer for 10 hours", "Two
Videographers for a full day".

**Decided:** option (b), the roles model, dual-written. A package now carries
`includedCoverage: {role, count}[]`; `features/packages/coverage.ts` is the only
way to read it, and answers for the old shape too. Nothing is migrated — package
documents gain the field the next time they are saved, and snapshots, being
immutable, keep the old shape forever, which is why the fallback read is
permanent rather than transitional. `includedPhotographers` is still written
beside it and `assertCoverageConsistent` refuses a write where the two disagree.

Adding a third role (drone, photo booth) is one line in `coverageRoleSchema`
plus its label.

**The retainer decision, made deliberately:** `per_crew_member` could mean
everyone or photographers only on a multi-role package, and either default
silently repriced somebody. The rule now names the roles it bills for
(`billedRoles`), the studio picks them on the form, and **a rule naming none
keeps billing photographers** — so no package written before this change moves
by a cent. Pinned by `tests/package-coverage.test.ts`.

What moved with it:
- Create and Edit package both take the two counts; **Edit could not change
  coverage at all before**, which mattered because Gabe's packages already
  exist and would otherwise have had to be recreated.
- `functions/src/studio-import/native-assets.ts` now keeps the videographer
  count it had been parsing and throwing away. Its own test asserted two
  *photographers* for a fixture reading "Two GR Productions Videographers" —
  the bug, written down as an expectation. Corrected.
- The contract reader (`functions/src/ai/signed-agreement.ts`) extracts
  videographers, and the booking import form takes them.
- The couple's portal and the proposal render one line per role instead of
  "2 photographer(s)"; the AI draft surfaces get the coverage written out so a
  draft never calls a videographer a photographer.
- The crew cascade suggests one role per person still to book — a video-led
  package used to suggest nobody.
- The readiness "second shooter" check is now "is there crew still to book",
  which a photographer + videographer package failed before.

**Not done:** booking a package still does not invite that crew automatically;
the cascade is pre-filled and the studio starts the offers. See `roadmap.md`.

### F4 — When a job stops, so does its crew — SHIPPED (2026-09-19)
Bigger than the archive case it was found through: `crm/commands.ts` owns both
`transitionProject` and `archiveProject` and **never touched
`crewAssignments`**, so *calling off a wedding* left every offer standing too —
including an accepted one belonging to a second shooter holding the date, told
nothing.

Now `features/crew/job-stopped.ts` (mirrored into functions):

- **Cancelling** withdraws every live assignment. Somebody who never accepted
  is withdrawn quietly — the offer vanishing from their portal is the whole
  message. Somebody who **accepted** is withdrawn *and* emailed
  (`crew_assignment_cancelled`), carrying the studio's reason, which
  `transitionProject` already requires for a called-off job.
- **Postponing** drops the offers nobody answered and **keeps** an accepted
  one: the date is moving, not gone, and re-agreeing it is a conversation
  rather than a side effect.
- **Archiving refuses** while anyone is waiting — the same rule archiving a
  *client* already follows. Archiving stays allowed at any stage otherwise,
  which is what it is for; it just cannot hide a job with a live offer on it.
  The refusal names cancelling as the move that actually ends them.
- A cascade still working down its list is closed, so it cannot offer the next
  name on a job that has stopped.

**Sent, not drafted** — a change from the recommendation. Crew mail in this
product is transactional and already auto-sends (the invitation, the reminder),
there is no crew draft-and-approve surface (`communicationDrafts` is
contact-shaped), and the harm of silence falls on a third party who has turned
other work down. Say so if you would rather it waited for approval.

**Latent bug fixed on the way:** the crew-profile archive guard tested
`settled = ["declined", "withdrawn", "completed", "closed"]`. `withdrawn` and
`closed` are not in `assignmentStatusSchema`; `expired`, `cancelled` and
`reassigned`, which are, were missing. So the three commonest ways an offer
ends counted as open and a collaborator whose assignments had all expired could
never be archived. Your production tenant is full of expired assignments.

### F4 (original note) — Archiving a job leaves its crew offers outstanding
Seen while cleaning up the walk. Both test jobs were archived; both crew offers
stayed `invited` and stayed listed under "Upcoming work" on the crew page. A
crew member holds an offer for a job the studio has put away, and the studio
sees it as live work.

Same shape as C1 (album reminders on an archived job), and `clientOutreachStop`
is the precedent — but that guard is about *client* mail and deliberately says
nothing about crew. Auto-offer makes this much more likely to bite, because
offers now leave at booking rather than when someone gets round to staffing.

**Decide:** does archiving withdraw outstanding offers, or only stop new ones?
Withdrawing is a message to a real person ("that job is off"), so it is not
obviously right to do silently.

### F5 — "Shooting this one solo" before a package is chosen — FIXED
The crew step counted as **done** on a brand-new enquiry. With no package there
is no coverage and so nobody to book, which is arithmetic rather than a
decision the studio has made — and the journey was ticking a box on its behalf,
and inflating the progress count. `packageChosen` now gates the solo reading;
a caller that does not pass it is unchanged.

### F5 (original note) — "Shooting this one solo" before a package is chosen
A new job with no package locked shows the crew step as settled: "Crew
confirmed · Shooting this one solo". It is technically consistent — no package
means no coverage means nobody to book — but it states a decision the studio
has not made yet. It corrects itself the moment a package is locked.
